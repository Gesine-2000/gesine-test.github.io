Research Methods and Professional Practice

Summary

The Research Methods and Professional Practice provides a critical foundation for designing, evaluating, and applying research within academic and professional contexts. It explores qualitative and quantitative research methodologies and their role in generating robust, evidence-based insights to support informed decision-making. The module develops an understanding of research design, data collection techniques, and analytical approaches, with a strong emphasis on ethical considerations and academic integrity. I developed the ability to critically evaluate existing literature and research outputs, ensuring methodological rigour and relevance to organisational and professional challenges. The module further emphasises reflective professional practice, strengthening academic writing, critical thinking, and the application of research findings to real-world and enterprise-level contexts.

Reflective Activities Across the Module
All reflective activities completed throughout the module are evidenced through linked artefacts, discussion posts, seminar outputs, and short reflections.

Units 1–3: Foundations of Research & Data

The early units focused on foundational research concepts, including the purpose of research, deductive versus inductive reasoning, and ethical considerations in computing. Activities such as the Reasoning Quiz, Reflective Activity on Ethics, and Collaborative Discussion 1 required engagement with ethical dilemmas and workplace scenarios, which were documented in discussion posts and short reflections. Through these tasks, I developed a clearer understanding of how research questions are shaped by epistemological assumptions and ethical constraints. Peer interaction during the collaborative discussion exposed me to alternative perspectives and helped refine my own arguments. Independent learning was demonstrated through additional reading on research ethics and methodology, which informed my early topic selection and supported the development of my initial literature review and research proposal outline.

Units 4–6: Research Design, Methods & Ethics

These units emphasised research design, qualitative and quantitative methods, and ethical data collection. Key activities included case-study seminars, literature review outline submission, reflective work on inappropriate survey use, and a wiki activity on questionnaire design. This stage strengthened my ability to critically assess methodological suitability rather than treating methods as interchangeable. I was able to relate interview and survey design principles to prior academic experience and to professional tasks involving training and feedback surveys. Independent working was evidenced through revisiting methodological decisions made during my undergraduate thesis and consulting additional sources on survey bias and validity to improve my research proposal structure.

Unit 7: Proposal Development & Statistical Foundations

Unit 7 marked a transition from research design to applied analysis. Activities included the Inferential Statistics Workshop, associated worksheets, and Collaborative Discussion 2 on the accuracy of information. These artefacts are linked within the portfolio. Engaging with inferential statistics challenged my existing skill set, particularly hypothesis testing and interpretation of outputs. Practical Excel-based exercises supported applied learning and helped translate abstract statistical concepts into usable analytical techniques. Independent learning was demonstrated through repeated experimentation with the worksheets and further reading on statistical accuracy and validity, which informed both my discussion contributions and the refinement of my research proposal.

For Unit 8 & 9 please see “Statistics Exercises & Worksheets”

Units 10–12: Research Writing, Professional Development & Synthesis

The final units focused on research communication, professional development, and consolidation of learning across the module. Key activities included submission of all statistical worksheets, Assignment 2 (including a recorded presentation and transcript), e-Portfolio preparation, and a self-test quiz. These tasks reinforced the importance of translating analytical and statistical findings into clear, structured academic and professional outputs. Preparing the presentation for Assignment 2 drew on existing professional experience with management communication while requiring careful alignment with academic standards. Independent working was demonstrated through iterative refinement of written and presentation materials, synthesis of feedback from earlier units, and critical review of my overall learning progression to ensure coherence between research design, analysis, and communication.

Evaluation of Literature Review & Research Proposal

Assignment 1

My literature review (Assignment 1) demonstrated a strong understanding of the gender pay gap within Singapore’s IT sector, effectively situating the issue within both local and international labour-market contexts. A key strength was the critical engagement with quantitative evidence, particularly the distinction between adjusted and unadjusted pay gap measures and the implications of occupational segregation, promotion bottlenecks, and pay structures. Rather than presenting statistics descriptively, the review evaluated their explanatory limits and triangulated them with organisational and policy literature.

The integration of Human Capital Theory (Becker, 1993) and Gender Inequality Theory (Ridgeway, 2011) provided a coherent analytical framework. While Human Capital Theory was shown to explain part of the wage disparity, the review critically demonstrated its limitations in accounting for persistent structural and cultural barriers in the IT sector. This theoretical comparison strengthened the overall criticality and analytical depth of the review.

The literature base was broad and of high quality, combining peer-reviewed research with government data and reputable organisational reports. Sources were generally well integrated and appropriate for postgraduate study.

Reflecting on tutor feedback, the main area for improvement relates to clarity and focus. At times, the review became overly technical when multiple datasets or initiatives were discussed simultaneously. In future work, I will clearer prioritise the most influential factors. .

Assigment 2

My research proposal (Assignment 2) built directly on the analytical foundations developed in Assignment 1 by moving from literature synthesis to research design. A key strength was the clear translation of theory into empirical investigation. By structuring the project around a comparison between Human Capital Theory (Becker, 1993) and Gender Inequality Theory (Ridgeway, 2011), the proposal demonstrated how competing explanations could be systematically tested rather than discussed only at a conceptual level. This strengthened the coherence between research questions, theoretical framework, and methodology.

The proposal also showed an increased awareness of applied research impact. Positioning the study within Singapore’s Smart Nation strategy and proposing outputs such as an analytics dashboard and policy brief reflected an ability to communicate research in ways relevant to professional stakeholders. Tutor feedback confirmed that the presentation was clear, professional, and logically structured, particularly the visualisation of the mixed-methods design.

Methodologically, the integration of quantitative labour market data with qualitative document analysis represented a significant development in my research skills. I demonstrated an understanding of how different forms of evidence can be triangulated to explore both patterns of inequality and the organisational mechanisms behind them.

Reflecting on feedback, the main area for improvement concerns visual communication. Some slides relied too heavily on text, particularly in the literature section, where thematic or chronological visuals could have improved clarity. In future work, I will focus on strengthening data visualisation to ensure that complex evidence is communicated more effectively and professionally.

Professional Skills Development

My professional skills development is evidenced through a Skills Matrix , a SWOT Analysis, and an evolving Action Plan . The Professional Skills Matrix reflects competencies developed across the duration of my MSc programme, rather than being limited to this module alone. It therefore provides a holistic overview of my current skill profile at this stage of the degree.

My Professional Skills matrix highlights established strengths in analytical and structured thinking, enterprise IT understanding, research awareness, and stakeholder communication. These capabilities have been progressively developed through multiple modules and applied within both academic and professional contexts. Within this specific module, these skills were further reinforced through critical engagement with research methods, evaluation of statistical evidence, and structured analysis of methodological limitations.

My SWOT Analysis supports this overview by identifying current development gaps, most notably limited hands-on proficiency in advanced coding and statistical implementation, as well as time constraints when balancing academic and professional responsibilities. These gaps are particularly relevant to research-intensive and enterprise data analysis contexts, where technical execution increasingly complements conceptual and managerial expertise.

My Action Plan, originally developed earlier in the programme, is being adapted and extended to reflect learning from this module. Planned actions include targeted upskilling in Python and applied statistics, increased practical experimentation with data analysis tools, and the pursuit of relevant professional certifications. The revised plan focuses on strengthening technical depth while continuing to apply research and analytical skills within a professional enterprise environment.
